# "How National Coffee Chains Enter Regional Markets"

## Summary
Fictional industry market note (July 2026) on how national coffee chains enter new regions and how
local roasters respond. External benchmark for the Daybreak brief — not a Daybreak-specific source.

## Key findings
- Entrants typically open two or three flagship stores, then decide on a wider rollout after a
  quarter; those that hit traffic targets in the first 90 days reached 6–10 stores within a year
  [Market Note, ¶1].
- Opening-quarter prices on drip and espresso drinks averaged 10–15% below local specialty roasters,
  often with an app offer or a free first subscription month, and moved back toward the local market
  within two quarters [Market Note, ¶2].
- Local roasters that matched the entrant's prices lost margin without keeping more regulars; those
  that held prices and focused on freshness, origin and loyalty lost fewer regulars [Market Note, ¶3].
- The first 90 days are the decisive window; a late response meets customer habits already set
  [Market Note, ¶4].
