REGIONAL LOGISTICS MARKET NOTE
"Depot Consolidation Trends Among Mid-Size Regional Carriers"
Published: June 2026 — Fictional industry publication, for demo purposes only

Mid-size regional trucking and last-mile carriers (typically 2–5 depots, sub-500 tractor fleets)
have shown a steady trend toward depot consolidation over the past three years, driven primarily by
facility lease renewal cycles rather than by planned network redesign — operators tend to treat a
lease expiry as the forcing event that triggers a consolidation review, rather than initiating one
proactively.

Industry surveys of carriers that consolidated from three sites to two found average fixed-cost
savings in the 8-14% range, concentrated in facility lease/utilities and site-level administrative
headcount, partially offset by increased line-haul cost between the remaining sites. Carriers that
attempted single-site consolidation from three depots reported a wider outcome range (savings from
8% to as much as 22%, but with three cases of documented delivery-window degradation in outlying
service areas serious enough to trigger customer churn).

A recurring finding across the surveyed cases: carriers that modeled only a single-site scenario,
without a two-site fallback, were more likely to report the delivery-coverage degradation outcome —
attributed to insufficient stress-testing of the base-case site's capacity assumptions before
committing. Carriers that modeled both single- and two-site scenarios in parallel were more likely
to catch capacity shortfalls before implementation rather than after.

Facility lease timing is flagged repeatedly as an underweighted risk factor: several case studies
describe a consolidation study still in progress when an individual site's own lease deadline forced
a standalone decision on that site ahead of the broader study's conclusions — decoupling that one
site's fate from the rest of the analysis, sometimes to the client's benefit and sometimes not,
depending on how much slack existed in the broader timeline.
